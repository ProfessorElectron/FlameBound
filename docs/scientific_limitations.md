# Scientific Limitations

- The project is a research triage and evidence-mapping tool. It does not certify materials or determine whether a habitat is safe.
- NASA investigations may use different fuels, sample geometries, flow configurations, sensors, and outcome definitions. Combining them requires an explicit comparability review.
- Missing or ambiguous source information must remain unknown or ambiguous; it must not be converted into a negative outcome.
- Microgravity and 1 g observations do not establish behaviour at lunar or other partial gravity. No partial-gravity interpolation is planned without relevant observations and validation.
- Oxygen partial pressure is derived from oxygen mole fraction and total pressure. The three should not be treated as independent predictors without a reasoned statistical design.
- Coverage distances and convex-hull flags describe geometric representation in selected variables. They are not calibrated probabilities and do not imply safety.
- Statistical spread estimates, if supported by the dataset, are conditional empirical estimates. Their validity depends on data quantity, label quality, predictor overlap, and investigation-held-out validation.
- Simplified dimensionless groups and flame-spread scalings require physical properties and regime assumptions that may not be reported in PSI. They must not be calculated by silently substituting assumed values.
- Any model result must show its inputs, units, evidence IDs, uncertainty where supported, and domain limitations.
